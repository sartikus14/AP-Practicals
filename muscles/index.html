      Telling the cat chest apart

      • Work from the surface down: pectoantebrachialis, then pectoralis major, then pectoralis minor, then xiphihumeralis.
      • The cat pectoralis minor is larger than the major. That is the reverse of humans, and a common trick question.
      • Latissimus dorsi wraps from the back around to the armpit, just behind the xiphihumeralis.

      Back and abdomen

      • Abdominal layers, outside in: external oblique, internal oblique, transversus abdominis. Rectus abdominis runs up the midline.
      • External oblique fibers run like hands in front pockets (down and toward the midline). Internal oblique fibers run at a right angle to them.
      • Transversus abdominis fibers run horizontally.
      • The cat rectus abdominis is hard to see without cutting the obliques, which is why your sheet flags it.

      Thigh and leg

      • Sartorius is the long strap crossing the front of the thigh. On the cat it is a wide band on the medial side.
      • Gracilis is the wide flat sheet covering the medial thigh in the cat. In humans it is a thin strap.
      • Hamstrings, lateral to medial: biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus.
      • Quadriceps on your sheet: rectus femoris (middle front), vastus lateralis (outer), vastus medialis (inner).
      • Gastrocnemius is the two-headed surface calf muscle. Soleus is flat and lies under it.
