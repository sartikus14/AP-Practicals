    Missed this session

      Telling left from right

      • Humerus: head points medial, greater tubercle is lateral, olecranon fossa is posterior.
      • Femur: head points medial, linea aspera is posterior, patellar surface is anterior.
      • Scapula: glenoid cavity faces lateral, spine is on the back, coracoid points forward.
      • Tibia: medial malleolus is medial, tibial tuberosity faces anterior.
      • Fibula: the lateral malleolus's smooth joint facet faces medial toward the talus.
      • Clavicle: flat end goes lateral, the rough tubercle and grooves are on the underside.
      • Os coxae: acetabulum faces lateral, pubis is anterior, iliac crest is up.

      Same name, different bone

      • Coronoid process: mandible (front of ramus) and ulna (below trochlear notch).
      • Coronoid fossa: humerus, anterior, catches the ulna's coronoid process.
      • Styloid process: temporal, radius, and ulna.
      • Auricular surface: sacrum and ilium, the two sides of the SI joint.
      • Head: humerus, radius, ulna (distal!), femur, fibula, rib.
      • Condyles: occipital, mandible, femur, tibia.

      Which vertebra is it?

      • Holes in the transverse processes: cervical.
      • No body, just a ring: atlas (C1).
      • Tooth sticking up: axis (C2).
      • Rib facets on the body, long downward spinous process: thoracic.
      • Huge kidney-shaped body, short square spinous process: lumbar.

      Mnemonics

      • Carpals, proximal row lateral to medial, then distal row: Some Lovers Try Positions That They Can't Handle (scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform, trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate).
      • Trapezium sits under the thumb: "the thumb is the M."
      • Tarsals: Tiger Cubs Need MILC (talus, calcaneus, navicular, medial, intermediate, lateral cuneiforms, cuboid).
      • Sphenoid foramina front to back: rotundum, ovale, spinosum (R-O-S, small to big then tiny).

      Station habits

      • First ask which bone you're looking at, then find the pin. Narrows it fast.
      • Check if the pin is on a hole, a bump, a groove, or a smooth surface. The word usually follows (foramen, process or tubercle, sulcus, facet).
      • Write the full name and spell it right. "Process" vs "tuberosity" matters.
      • If the question asks for side, say left or right.
